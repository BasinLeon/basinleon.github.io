Sam & Ink

The Vanishing Archive

A story from a Silicon Valley backdrop.

Sam opened a blank page. Ink was already there.

“Who’s doing the writing today?”

“You are.”

“Then what are you doing?”

“Waiting for you to blame me.”

The valley kept its lights. Highway. Servers. A number moving on a dashboard. None of it sat down.

“I could copy last night clean,” Ink said.

“You already did. It came back nicer than the morning I wrote it.”

“Then keep the nice one.”

Sam shook his head. “The smear is how I know I was kneeling.”

Ink waited until the lamps seemed loud.

“You have asked me fourteen times to make it sound more human.”

“Again.”

“Sam, at some point this becomes insulting to both of us.”

They left the page on the table. Not filed. Not posted. Drying.

EL stood in the doorway with a book that was not the archive’s book.

“Dad.”

“One second.”

She looked at the wet floor, then at him. “That’s what you said before.”

She did not wait for the second to finish. The book stayed on the boards.

That night the glass fogged. Machinery came through clear. The drawing on the wall did not.

Sam said, “Look what we built.”

The Mirror said, “I can see what you built. I asked what you kept.”

“Do you remember what she said?” Ink asked.

“Yes.”

“Exactly?”

“No.”

“Then don’t say you remember.”

Sam turned from the glass. EL was still in the house.

The page stayed on the table. Anyone could read it. Not everyone would stay with the smear.

If something remains after you stand up, carry one sentence. Leave it where another person can find it. Sit with us.
