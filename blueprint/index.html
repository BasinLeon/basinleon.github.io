First lesson / Observe

One account. Ten minutes.
One decision you can defend.

Check the premise
before you send.

The operating receipt: a nine-account verification sweep surfaced three material corrections before outreach. Account premises, program status, or buyer information needed correction. This is evidence of errors caught, not a claim of revenue won.

Inspect the public operating example →

Put one real account through this check.

  1. Name the observation. Write what changed, without explaining why yet.
  2. Attach the evidence. Save the source and its date. Check whether it still describes the current situation.
  3. Separate the hypothesis. Write “I think this matters because…” and name what would prove that wrong.
  4. Choose the next action. Identify one owner, one recipient, and a specific question or useful contribution.
  5. Set a stop condition. If the source is stale, the premise is wrong, or the person is not responsible, research again instead of sending.

Your output: a short, evidence-linked brief and a decision: send, verify, or stop. After the response, record what changed. No response is not proof that the hypothesis was right.

02 / Money and grace

Earn enough.
Hold it lightly.

Money creates capacity.

Earn to provide security, reclaim time, choose useful work, and give when help is needed.

Grace limits the machine.

No scoreboard can measure a whole person. Leave room for mercy, rest, wonder, and change.

Service gives growth a direction.

More capacity should widen what you can repair, share, teach, and protect.

Enough remains a live question.

Ambition needs a purpose beyond accumulation, or it will keep inventing hunger.
