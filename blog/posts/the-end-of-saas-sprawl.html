Edition 39: The End of SaaS Sprawl

Adding more point tools does not fix a broken funnel. Modern GTM runs on signal routing, context, and clean handoffs.

Leon Basin · March 4, 2026

Leon Basin

Commercial Operations | GTM Operating Model Leader | Scaling Exe…

Most GTM teams I meet are paralyzed by their own technology.

Founders and revenue leaders spend tens of thousands of dollars every month on intent data providers, sequencing tools, enrichment platforms, and dialing software.

Despite that massive investment, reps are still sending generic outbound messages. Account executives still show up to discovery calls flying blind.

I call this SaaS Sprawl. It destroys efficiency.

The hard truth about B2B growth right now is that your team does not need another tool. They need an integrated operating model.

The Specificity Gap

For the last five years, the playbook was simple. You bought a massive platform and forced your team to match its rigid constraints. If the software could not do it, you did not do it.

But vendors leave out one major detail. Their intent data is the exact same data your three closest competitors buy. You get the same signal at the same time and trigger the exact same generic email.

You are not buying an edge. You are buying a commodity and calling it a strategy.

To win today, you must be specific and highly relevant. You cannot do that when a vendor in another timezone owns your signal logic.

If your sales rep is sending an email that says "Hope your Tuesday is going well", you have already lost. You lost to the operator whose system triggered an outreach the exact second a target company posted a new job listing for a Kubernetes engineer.

The Rise of the Signal Engine

The best GTM operators no longer buy rigid subscriptions. They build Signal Engines.

A Signal Engine is a custom pipeline using infrastructure like Clay or n8n that actually hunts for data instead of just storing it. It watches for specific buying triggers like funding rounds or leadership changes. Then it automatically enriches that data with context before a rep ever sees it.

Instead of paying a massive monthly retainer for a platform that guesses at intent, modern revenue architects define their own logic and pay only for verified outcomes.

This is the difference between having a tech stack and having a real commercial operating model. One is a cost center. The other generates revenue.

The Architect

Here is a quick test to see where you stand.

Ask yourself who on your team owns the logic between your data and your pipeline.

I do not mean the tool or the dashboard. I mean the actual business rules that decide what signal matters, at what threshold, and how it gets routed to a rep.

If you cannot name that person in three seconds, you do not have a revenue architect.

You have a very expensive subscription manager.

The difference comes down to whether your GTM motion is being designed or just maintained. Administrators keep the lights on. Architects wire the building.

A Signal Engine is not a product you install. It is an operating model someone has to build from the trigger logic down to the scoring model. That work requires a person who can read a business objective, translate it into a data architecture, and execute it in code.

Most organizations lack that person. That is a talent problem, not a software problem.

The era of throwing subscriptions at a growth problem is over. The question is not whether you should build a Signal Engine. The question is whether you have someone who can.
